# Eastside Hockey Manager – Pre-Set Tactics Guide

This guide documents three community-created pre-set tactics for Eastside Hockey Manager: the Simple Tactic, the Slapshot Tactic, and the RED PlaymOver Barrage Tactic. Each section sets out the full tactical configuration, the player attributes required to run the tactic effectively, community-reported results, known weaknesses, and suggested tweaks. All three tactics are high-scoring, offensively minded approaches and are suitable as starting points for players who want a proven framework to build from rather than constructing a tactic from scratch. The guide is primarily applicable to EHM 2007 and EHM 1 (Steam).

This summary has been prepared by Claude AI based on a review of the source forum threads from ehmtheblueline.com and set out in the `Sources` section at the end of this guide.

## 1. Overview and Philosophy

All three tactics in this guide share a broadly similar philosophy: prioritise offence, generate a high volume of shots, and trust in superior goal-scoring to offset defensive vulnerability. Each has different degrees of aggression and complexity:

- **Simple Tactic** — the most conservative of the three, designed to be accessible and reliable across all leagues and skill levels. Based on speed and sound positional play.
- **Slapshot Tactic** — a community classic, one of EHM's most widely circulated preset tactics. A structured four-line system with differentiated line roles and an integrated PP setup.
- **RED PlaymOver Barrage** — the most extreme of the three. Maximum offensive pressure across all four lines. Produces spectacular attacking output but requires good players and a strong goalie to survive defensively.

All three work across a wide range of leagues and are not limited to the NHL. Community reports include success in the AHL, CHL, ECHL, KHL, SEL, EBEL, and various European leagues.

## 2. The Simple Tactic

### Concept

The Simple Tactic is designed around real-life hockey principles: skill and speed over size, positional play, rolling lines evenly, and letting fast players create and exploit counter-attacking opportunities. Its creator described the inspiration as the Mike Babcock defensive possession system — cut off passing lanes, recover the puck, and push it up the ice quickly. The result is a tactic that is defensively solid through speed and backchecking rather than through physical shutdown play, and offensively dangerous through quick transitions and creative passing.

It uses **team-wide tactics** (no unit tactics) intentionally, keeping management simple.

### Player Requirements

The defining requirement is **speed across the entire roster** — forwards and defensemen alike. Target the following for all players, including defensemen:

- **Speed:** 13 minimum, ideally 15+
- **Acceleration:** 13 minimum, ideally 15+
- **Stickhandling:** 13 minimum
- **Passing:** 13 minimum

Size, physical strength, and fighting ability are explicitly secondary to these four attributes. The tactic does not rely on a dominant first line — instead, it works best when scoring is distributed evenly across all four lines, so that no single line can be shut down.

### Tactical Settings

Applied team-wide (no unit tactics):

| Setting | Value |
|---|---|
| Gap Control | Tight |
| Backchecking | Hard |
| Passing | Creative |
| Dumping the Puck | Rarely |

All other settings left at defaults. The Positional defensive zone, forecheck, and neutral zone settings are intentionally kept at their game defaults.

> ⚠️ Creative passing requires players with decent Creativity and Passing attributes to avoid generating turnovers. If your players have lower creativity (below ~13), consider dropping Passing to Normal to reduce giveaways — this was the most commonly cited tweak in community posts.

### Personal Tactics

- **Shoot/Pass tendency:** Set based on each player's best attribute — snipers to Shoot, playmakers to Pass.
- **Carry the Puck:** Enabled for all players (the tactic relies on fast players carrying the puck up ice).
- **Join the Rush:** Enabled for all players.
- **Hitting:** Enabled (Hard) only for players with sufficient Hitting and Aggressiveness attributes.
- **Shooting frequency:** Only deviate from the default for a clear sniper (set them slightly more aggressive) or a clear distributor (set them to favour passing).

One community-developed enhancement is to place **one defensively responsible forward** on each line with their mentality set one click lower than the line's default, and one more defensive defenseman per pair who does not join the rush and backchecks on Very Hard. This forward and defenseman are then set to check the opponent's top two scorers before each game, which the original author described as "killing their goal-scoring" against one-line wonder opponents.

### Practice Recommendations

The original author's practice setup: all players on **Intensive Conditioning, Skating, Offensive Skills, and Defensive Skills; everything else Medium**. Coaches are spread across all practice types with extra priority on Skating — reflecting the speed requirements of the tactic.

### Community Results and Tweaks

The tactic was tested across the NHL, AHL, ECHL, CHL (OHL and QMJHL), KHL, WHL, and British hockey. The original author reported finishing first in every league tested. Community members reported success in the SHL, Swedish HockeyAllsvenskan, SPHL, and lower European leagues.

Consistent community observations:
- The tactic tends to produce strong shot generation — typical seasons saw the team outshoot opponents by significant margins.
- Even distribution of scoring across four lines meant that no single player's slump derailed the team.
- Players with high creative passing attributes (15+ Creativity) benefit most from the Creative passing setting.

**The most effective reported enhancement** (developed in the community discussion) combines the Simple Tactic with:
- **Shot targeting** — setting Low or Five Hole depending on the opposing goalie's weakness, switching if the first approach is not working.
- **Checking the opponent's top two scorers** each game using the Player to Check personal tactic setting.
- **Offensive Zone set to Free Flowing / Grouped / Overload Slot** — described as forcing cross-crease chances by getting the opposite winger deeper into the slot.
- **Tempo set to High for the top six forwards** and **Very High for the bottom six and defensemen** (who have less ice time and can sustain the higher pace more easily).

Reported results with this enhanced version: 6 cups in 7 seasons, 7 Presidents' Trophies, 320+ goals every season, and sub-200 goals against in some seasons.

### Known Weaknesses and Mitigations

- **Goaltending is exposed** — the tactic is not a defensive shutdown system. A weak goalie will concede frequently. A quality starting goalie is strongly recommended.
- **Creative passing increases turnovers** — when this becomes an issue (particularly against highly skilled opponents), dropping Passing to Normal for the playoffs stabilised results for multiple community users.
- **Junior leagues can be difficult** — the AI receives development bonuses in junior hockey and star player slumps can be more pronounced. Balancing ice time evenly and using overage slots on goaltending are workarounds that helped.

## 3. The Slapshot Tactic

### Concept

The Slapshot Tactic is one of the oldest and most widely circulated tactics in the EHM community, originally documented in the SI forums and later preserved and shared on ehmtheblueline.com. It uses **unit tactics** with differentiated settings for each of the four forward lines, creating a structured system where the top two lines play in an offensive, skill-based style, the third line transitions to a two-way/physical role, and the fourth line plays a defensive/checking game. The PP is fully integrated into the tactic. It was designed to work as a starting point — a solid, proven foundation that players can adapt over time.

### Player Requirements

- **Centres:** Strong overall players; the tactic is built around the centre as the primary offensive driver. Faceoffs, creativity, passing, and skating are all important.
- **Wingers (Lines 1–2):** Speed, Acceleration, Stickhandling, Deking, Getting Open. Offensive skill over physicality.
- **Wingers (Lines 3–4):** More physical and two-way. Checking, Strength, Balance. Can dump the puck.
- **Defensemen (Pair 1):** Strong across the board — Checking, Positioning, Pokecheck, Stamina, Strength, and good offensive attributes (Slapshot, Wristshot).
- **Defensemen (Pairs 2–3):** Solid overall stats across all important defensive attributes. Balance and consistency over star quality.
- **Goaltender:** Technical attributes 17–18+ across the board.

### Personal Tactics

- **Goalies:** Defensive mentality, Safe passing.
- **Offensive defensemen:** Normal mentality, Normal passing.
- **Defensive defensemen:** Defensive mentality, Safe passing.
- **Wingers:** Join the Rush enabled.
- All other personal tactic settings are described as optional and player-specific.

### Community Results and Tweaks

The Slapshot Tactic was confirmed to work in EHM 2007 by multiple community members. Reported first-season performances include a 102-point Tavares, a 120-point Havlat leading the NHL in scoring, and cup wins in the first season. One player reported five PP goals in a single period. A second-season decline was frequently reported, attributed to the AI adapting to the tactic — the standard fix is to periodically vary Breakouts and Neutral Zone Offensive settings (without changing the core tactic) to prevent the AI from reading the approach.

The tactic was recommended as an **excellent starting point for new players** — solid enough to learn on, simple enough to modify, and transparent enough that changes are easy to test.

**Key community-identified tweaks:**
- Adjust Aggressiveness and Hitting down one notch if penalty counts are too high.
- Set up PK using the guidance in the dedicated Tactics threads on ehmtheblueline.com, as the original tactic has no PK instructions.
- Vary Breakouts (Free Flowing ↔ Positional ↔ Crisscross) regularly to prevent the AI adapting.
- In lower leagues (weaker goalies), switching to Heavy or Barrage shooting on Lines 1–2 can increase goal output.

One user noted the tactic was still working effectively as late as 2022 in EHM 2007, with the only ongoing management requirement being periodic breakout changes.

### Known Weaknesses and Mitigations

- **No PK defined** — the tactic requires PK setup to be added. Without it, the PK will default to team tactics, which produces poor results. This is the most commonly cited problem.
- **AI adaptation** — like all tactics, the AI will begin to counter it after multiple seasons. Change Breakouts and Neutral Zone Offensive settings every 5–10 games.
- **Young / developing players** — the tactic performs inconsistently with young players whose attributes are not yet developed. Results improve significantly once the core players reach their attribute targets.
- **Open defensive zone (Line 1)** — the Open defensive zone setting on Line 1 can leave defenders out of position. Some players switched this to Positional without noticeable offensive impact.

## 4. The RED PlaymOver Barrage Tactic

### Concept

The RED PlaymOver Barrage Tactic — whose name was interpreted in the community as "Play 'em Over" (i.e. overwhelm the opposition) — is an extreme high-pressure, high-volume offensive system that applies the same attacking settings uniformly across all four forward lines. The core philosophy is to win the puck, find the fastest passing option, and shoot immediately. All four lines play offensively, with the burden of defence shared across the whole roster rather than assigned to checking-line specialists. The tactic uses **unit tactics** but applies nearly identical settings to every line.

### Player Requirements

The creator specified the following roster requirements for top performance:

- **At least three strong centres** — the tactic is built around the centre as a creative playmaker. All four centres taking faceoffs is standard.
- **Pacy wingers** across all lines (Speed, Acceleration, Balance, Agility, Getting Open, Wristshot, Slapshot, Deking, Stickhandling, Work Rate)
- **One Norris-calibre defenseman** on the top pair
- **Very good second pair defensemen** — solid across all key attributes (Checking, Positioning, Pokecheck, Stamina, Strength, Speed)
- **A strong goaltender** — the tactic generates significant defensive exposure and a quality goalie is described as essential by the creator and multiple community members

### Team Options

| Setting | Value |
|---|---|
| Forward Usage | Normal |
| Defense Usage | Normal |
| PP Usage | Favour 1st |
| Line Matching | 1v2, 2v1, 3v3, 4v4 |
| 3-on-3 | Use 2 Defensemen |
| Use Unit Tactics | Yes |

### Tactical Settings

The following settings apply to **all four forward lines** (differences are only in shift length):

| Setting | Value |
|---|---|
| Mentality | Very Offensive |
| Aggressiveness | Berserk |
| Backchecking | Very Hard |
| Gap Control | Very Tight |
| Puck Pressure | Very Heavy |
| Hitting | Very Hard |
| Tempo | Very High |
| Passing | Creative |
| Shooting | Barrage |
| Dumping the Puck | Normal |

> ⚠️ The combination of Berserk Aggressiveness, Very Hard Hitting, and Very Heavy Puck Pressure will generate penalties and injuries if player attributes do not support these settings. Several community members reported early injury problems, particularly in the first few games. The creator and experienced users noted that this settles as players adapt and the tactic beds in, but it is worth monitoring PIM totals closely in the first season. One community member reduced Aggressiveness and Hitting by one notch each to bring PIM down to acceptable levels without significantly reducing offensive output.

| Setting | Value |
|---|---|

| Aggressiveness | Normal |
| Backchecking | Normal |
| Shooting | Barrage (with Point Shot faceoff setup) |
| Passing | Creative |
| Tempo | Normal to High |

In the defensive pairing for PP: typically one defenseman set to join the rush and shoot (Point Shot faceoff), one to stay back. Where possible, both defensemen are set to shoot in PP situations.

### Penalty Kill Settings

From the original tactic:

| Setting | Value |
|---|---|
| Playmaker in PP Role | No (remove playmaker setting from all PK players) |
| Mentality | Defensive |
| Passing | Safe |
| Dumping the Puck | Often to Very Often |

The PK does not use a playmaker role. No further PK settings were specified in the original tactic. Community experience suggests the PK is the element most requiring personal attention and tweaking — see the general Tactics guides for detailed PK setup.

### Personal Tactics

All offensive players (forwards and offensive defensemen):
- **Carry the Puck:** Enabled (remove only for young players with underdeveloped attributes)
- **Join the Rush:** Enabled
- **Shoot Tendency:** Enabled for snipers and defensemen with high Slapshot

For a defensive pair: one defenseman set to join the rush, one to stay back.

> ⚠️ Personal tactics are noted to reset frequently when lineup changes are made (injuries, line rotations). The creator specifically flagged this: personal tactics must be re-applied after most roster changes.

### Community Results and Benchmarks

Reported by the creator across multiple seasons with strong NHL teams (EHM 2007):

| Metric | Typical Range |
|---|---|
| Regular season wins | 63–74 |
| Goals for (season) | 375–460+ |
| Goals against (season) | 155–247 |
| PP% | 17.0–27.8% |
| PK% | 80.1–91.8% |
| First line production | 170–200+ pts (top player), 145+ (second player) |
| Second line | 70–115 pts (top player) |
| Third/fourth line | ~25–40 pts per player |

One community member reported 571 goals for and 124 against in a single season with an elite team, with the top scorer posting 249 points. These figures are exceptional outliers from a very strong team, not typical performance.

For an average NHL team, the creator suggested expecting approximately 280–320 goals for, 17.5% PP, and 73.5% PK in the first season — with improvement as the roster develops and the player becomes more familiar with the tactic. Several community reports from the first 1–2 seasons showed more goals against than for, which gradually improved as line and tactic adjustments were made.

The tactic has been reported as successful in the NHL, AHL, SEL (Swedish Elite), DEL (German Elite), KHL, Norwegian Get-League, EBEL (Austrian Elite), British leagues, CHL (WHL and OHL), and international tournaments. The pattern across different leagues is consistent: high goal output, weaker defensive numbers, and a requirement for a good goalie and quality forwards to reach full potential.

### Known Weaknesses and Mitigations

**High goals against:** This is the tactic's defining weakness. It is explicitly a "score more than them" philosophy. The primary mitigation is a strong goalie — a franchise-level goaltender substantially reduces the exposure. Several community members noted that their GAA was 2.5–3.5 throughout the season but that the offensive output made this sustainable.

**Penalty trouble:** The combination of Berserk Aggressiveness, Very Hard Hitting, and Very Heavy Puck Pressure generates penalties. Mitigation options:
- Reduce Aggressiveness and Hitting by one notch each.
- Ensure PK is properly set up with Safe passing and Defensive mentality.
- Monitor and match the PP/PK training percentage split accordingly.

**Early-season inconsistency:** Most users reported wildly variable results in the first 10–30 games before the tactic settled. This appears to be normal — multiple players reported going from big wins to large losses in the same week, before stabilising into a dominant pattern. Patience and giving it at least 20–30 games before major adjustments is advised.

**AI adaptation:** As with all tactics, AI teams adapt over time. Vary breakouts periodically. The creator noted that after multiple seasons, line-matching schemes (1v3 / 3v1 in addition to the default) can help in the playoffs when the same opponent is faced repeatedly.

**Line-matching adjustment:** The default 1v2, 2v1, 3v3, 4v4 matching sends the second-strongest line against the opponent's best line. This is deliberately asymmetric — the intent is that the first line should dominate any matchup it receives. For teams with less depth, switching to 1v3, 3v1, 2v2, 4v4 to protect the first line from the opponent's best defensive unit is an alternative approach cited by community members.

### EHM 1 Compatibility

Multiple community members tested this tactic in EHM 1 and reported that it works, with some caveats:

- Offensive output is somewhat lower than in EHM 2007 — the extreme scoring figures (400+ goals, 200+ point players) are less reproducible in EHM 1.
- Goals against are higher relative to output.
- The tactic is described as "still a good tactic" but "muted in effectiveness" compared to EHM 2007.
- Tweaking is required to find the right balance for the specific team and league.

## 5. General Advice for Pre-Set Tactics

**Treat pre-sets as starting points, not fixed solutions.** All three tactics in this guide were designed to be adapted. The creators explicitly encouraged experimentation. The most successful community users are those who loaded the tactic, identified what was and was not working for their specific team, and made targeted adjustments.

**Give a tactic time before evaluating it.** Players adapting to a new system takes 5–20 games. Dramatic early results — good or bad — are not representative. Evaluate after at least 15–20 games.

**Vary the breakouts.** All three tactics are susceptible to AI adaptation over multiple seasons. Changing the Breakout and Neutral Zone Offensive settings periodically (every 5–10 games) is the most commonly recommended way to stay ahead of the AI without disrupting the core tactic.

**Add a proper PK.** Neither the Simple Tactic nor the Slapshot Tactic includes defined PK settings. The RED PlaymOver provides minimal PK settings. Setting up a proper PK — with Defensive mentality, Safe passing, Very Hard backchecking, Tight Box defensive zone, and players who do not join the rush — is a significant upgrade for all three tactics.

**Personal tactics are multipliers.** Community experience across all three tactics consistently showed that proper personal tactics (matching each player's settings to their actual attributes) produced substantial improvements — one player reported a 60% increase in performance, and another reported 1.7x the scoring on the first line after implementing personal tactics properly. This is the single highest-leverage adjustment available for any tactic.

**Shot targeting is a useful supplement.** Setting low shots or five-hole depending on the opposing goalie's weakness was cited by multiple players as a particularly effective in-game adjustment, especially when facing hot goalies in the playoffs.

**A quality goalie is non-negotiable for high-tempo offensive tactics.** All three tactics place offensive pressure on the goalie by nature of their attacking philosophy. A below-average goalie will be exposed regardless of how well the other elements are set up.
